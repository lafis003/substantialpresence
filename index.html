Am I a US Resident for Tax Purposes?

Use this free calculator to find out whether you're considered a US resident alien or nonresident alien under IRS rules — without having to read through dense tax publications. The tool walks you through the Substantial Presence Test, common visa exemptions (F-1, J-1, M-1, diplomatic), and exceptions like the Closer Connection Exception.

The Substantial Presence Test (183-Day Rule)

The IRS uses the Substantial Presence Test to determine whether a non-citizen must file as a resident alien. You meet the test — and are treated as a US tax resident — if you were present in the US for at least 31 days during the current year and the following weighted sum is 183 or more:

Days in current year × 1  +  Days in prior year × ⅓  +  Days two years ago × ⅙

F-1 and J-1 Visa Holders

Students on F-1 visas and their F-2 dependents are exempt from counting days toward the Substantial Presence Test for up to 5 calendar years (lifetime). After that, days count normally. J-1 students follow the same 5-year rule; J-1 teachers, researchers, and trainees are exempt for up to 2 calendar years in any 6-year period. All exempt individuals must file Form 8843 annually.

Closer Connection Exception

Even if you meet the Substantial Presence Test, you may qualify as a nonresident alien if you were present fewer than 183 days, maintained a tax home abroad, and had closer ties to another country. File Form 8840 to claim this exception.
